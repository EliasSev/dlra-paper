Master’s thesis

Two contributions

  1. Efficient Elvetun–Nielsen weights. A matrix-free rSVD computes \(W\) without ever forming the forward operator \(K\). A low-rank SVD \(K = U \Sigma V^T \) is obtained.
  2. DLRA-CG. Dynamical low-rank approximation with conjugate-gradient search directions, applied to this problem with the Elvetun–Nielsen weights.

The weights \(W\) are known.

What about the SVD of \(K\)?.

Thesis contribution 1: the rSVD makes the Elvetun–Nielsen weights computable without forming K; the SVD of K was a by-product we also used for speedups. Contribution 2: DLRA with CG for this problem and these weights. The paper focuses on contribution 2 and treats W and K's factors as inputs.

The problem

Find an interior source from boundary measurements

Elliptic PDE \(-\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla u) + cu = f\), no flux over the boundary. We see \(u\) only on the boundary and want \(f\).

\[\min_x\ \Phi(x) = \tfrac12\|Kx-y\|^2 + \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\|Wx\|^2\]

Ill-posed: \(K\) has fast-decaying singular values and a large null space. \(H = K^\top K + \lambda^2 W^\top W\) is SPD but badly conditioned (\(\kappa \approx 10^9\) at \(\lambda = 10^{-4}\)).

Test problems I (one source), II (three sources), III (L-shaped domain)

Norms are FE-weighted (mass matrices) in the real formulation; dropped here for readability. Mesh 64×64 for problems I and II.

Problem 1: location bias

Plain Tikhonov pulls every source to the boundary

True source (left) and standard Tikhonov, \(W = I\) (right)

Interior sources lie mostly in the null space of \(K\), so the minimum-norm solution damps them.

Elvetun-Nielsen weights scale each basis function by how visible it is to \(K\):

\[w_i = \frac{\|VV^\top e_i\|_M}{\|\phi_i\|_M}, \qquad W = \operatorname{diag}(w)\]
VV^T is the projection onto the complement of the null space of K. Weights are given input for the paper.

Problem 2: smoothing

Elvetun-Nielsen weighted Tikhonov finds the source location, but blurs them together

Problem II, weighted Tikhonov (exact \(K\), then rSVD with \(k=10\) and \(k=50\)). Locations are right, peaks reach only 0.5–0.6 of the true amplitude 1, and the sources merge into one connected region.

True sources are sparse, Tikhonov gives smooth solutions. This motivates looking for low-rank solutions instead.

The idea

Sparse sources are low-rank matrices

Reshape \(x\) into an \(n\times n\) matrix \(X\). One square source is one outer product (rank 1); problem II is rank 3. So we search only over rank-\(r\) matrices \(X = U\Sigma V^\top\), \(r \ll n\).

The rank r roughly tracks the number of distinct localized sources. Working with factors means 2nr unknowns instead of n².

The baseline

DLRA: take a step, stay on the rank-\(r\) manifold

<
  1. Choose a search direction \(D\)
  2. Update \(U, V\) and \(\Sigma\) along \(D\) (the “KLS” step of Schotthöfer et al.)
  3. Truncate back to rank \(r\)

Original method: steepest descent, Adam or fixed step with \(\kappa(H)\approx 10^9\) needs \( \approx 10000 \) iterations.

KLS = basis update and Galerkin integrator; bases augmented to 2r columns, then truncated. Robust, non-stiff (no inverse of Sigma).

The contribution

DLRA-CG

Using “CG” directions

\(\Phi\) is quadratic with SPD Hessian, so CG is natural. Keep the DLRA update, take \(D\) from the CG recurrence:

\[\alpha = \frac{\|G\|^2}{\langle D, HD\rangle}, \qquad D \leftarrow -G + \beta D\]

Same cost per iteration as steepest descent.

Restarting the CG

The recurrence updates the gradient along a full-rank path, not at the truncated iterate. They drift apart: the error falls, then rises (semi-convergence).

Restarted DLRA-CG: every \(N_r\) steps, recompute the true gradient at \(X\) and reset \(D = -G\).

Fletcher–Reeves beta. Gradient recurrence G_new = G + alpha H D. N_r = 1 is steepest descent; too large and semi-convergence returns. DLRA-PCG replaces G by P^-1 G as usual.

Since the summer

Riemannian steepest descent with exact line search

A low-rank method whose every step is taken from the true iterate

The one new item since the thesis. Three slides of theory, one comparison with what we had, then where the implementation stands.

Theory

Solve a linear matrix equation on the rank-\(r\) manifold

Consider a general equation with \(\mathcal L\) self-adjoint and SPD:

\[\mathcal L[X] = A_1X + XA_2 + A_3XA_4 = Y\]

For us, \(\mathcal L = H\) (the Tikhonov Hessian) and \(Y = \operatorname{mat}(K^\top y)\).

On \(\mathcal M_r\) we cannot ask for \(\mathcal L[X] = Y\). We ask for the projected condition

\[P(X)\,\big(\mathcal L[X] - Y\big) = 0,\]

the first-order optimality condition of \(\min_{X\in\mathcal M_r}\ \Phi(X) = \tfrac12\langle \mathcal L[X], X\rangle - \langle Y, X\rangle\).

Tangent projection at \(X = USV^\top\):
\(P(X)Z = UU^\top Z + ZVV^\top - UU^\top Z VV^\top\)

G[X] = L[X] − Y is the Euclidean gradient of Phi, the "defect". The Riemannian gradient is its tangent projection P(X)G. We assume the Hessian of Phi is SPD, which holds for our H.

Theory

Projected gradient, then the exact step length

1. Riemannian gradient

\[G_k = \mathcal L[X_k] - Y\]\[R_k = -P(X_k)\,G_k\]

\(\|R_k\|\) is the stopping criterion.

2. Direction

\[D_k = R_k\]

Already a tangent vector, so no extra projection is needed.

3. Exact line search

\(\Phi\) is quadratic, so minimize \(\Phi(X_k + \alpha D_k)\) in closed form:

\[\alpha_k = \frac{\|R_k\|_F^2}{\langle R_k, \mathcal L[R_k]\rangle}\]

Step 3 uses \(\langle G_k, D_k\rangle = \langle P(X_k)G_k, D_k\rangle = -\|R_k\|^2\), which holds because \(D_k\) is tangent.

The line search is along the straight line X + alpha D in the ambient space, before retraction, so it is exact for that line, not for the retracted point. Same alpha formula as CG uses, but with the projected gradient.

Theory

Retract with a KLS step, and keep everything thin

Retraction (DLRT update)

\[\begin{aligned} K' &= U_kS_k - \alpha_k\,G_kV_k, & \hat U &= \operatorname{orth}(K')\\ L' &= V_kS_k^\top - \alpha_k\,G_k^\top U_k, & \hat V &= \operatorname{orth}(L')\\ \hat S &= (\hat U^\top U_k)\,S_k\,(V_k^\top \hat V) - \alpha_k\,\hat U^\top G_k \hat V \end{aligned}\]

Then an \(r\times r\) SVD of \(\hat S\) gives \(U_{k+1}, S_{k+1}, V_{k+1}\). Bases stay rank \(r\).

Three thin factors

Everything above needs only \(U_k^\top G_k\), \(G_kV_k\) and \(U_k^\top G_kV_k\):

\[\|R_k\|^2 = \|U_k^\top G_k\|^2 + \|G_kV_k\|^2 - \|U_k^\top G_kV_k\|^2\]

For Sylvester-type \(\mathcal L\) these cost a few products with \(n\times r\) matrices, and no \(n\times n\) matrix is ever formed.

The denominator of alpha is computed from the same factors plus U^T L[D] and L[D] V. For our H (no Sylvester structure) the implementation applies H to a full n×n matrix instead.
